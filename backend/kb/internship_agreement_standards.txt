OVERVIEW

Internship agreements in India occupy an unusual legal space: they are neither classic employment contracts (which trigger the full suite of labor protections) nor purely civil contracts. The rights and obligations of interns in India are evolving rapidly, particularly after high-profile internship stipend disputes and the government's Prime Minister Internship Scheme (2024).

This KB covers what an internship agreement should and should not contain, market benchmarks, and legal protections available to interns.

---

LEGAL FRAMEWORK FOR INTERNSHIPS IN INDIA

There is NO dedicated central legislation governing private-sector internships in India as of 2024. Key reference points:

1. Contract Act, 1872: An internship agreement is a valid contract. Provisions on offer, acceptance, consideration, and breach apply.

2. Employees' State Insurance (ESI) Act, 1948: Amended in 2023 to include interns earning ≤ ₹21,000/month in companies covered by the ESI Act (20+ employees in most sectors). This means intern stipends trigger ESIC contributions in many cases.

3. Apprentices Act, 1961: Government-notified apprenticeships (under the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme — NAPS) have specific rights, stipends, and protections. Private internships not registered under NAPS are unregulated.

4. State Shops & Establishments Acts: Some state acts (e.g., Maharashtra) are being interpreted to include interns in specific categories — evolving area.

5. PM Internship Scheme (2024): Central government launched a scheme providing 1-year internships at top-500 companies in India. ₹5,000/month stipend (₹4,500 from government + ₹500 from company). One-time ₹6,000 incidental allowance. Interns receive accident insurance under PM Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana and PM Suraksha Bima Yojana. Companies must provide skill development alongside the internship.

INTERNSHIP STIPEND STANDARDS (2024, INDIA)

By sector:
- FAANG / global product companies (Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta): ₹60,000–₹1,50,000/month
- Indian unicorns and funded startups (Razorpay, CRED, Meesho, Zepto): ₹25,000–₹60,000/month
- IT services (TCS, Infosys, Wipro): ₹10,000–₹20,000/month
- Consulting (Big 4 — Deloitte, EY, PwC, KPMG): ₹30,000–₹50,000/month
- MBA summer internships (IIM graduates, top B-schools): ₹80,000–₹2,00,000/month
- Engineering campus internships (IITs, NITs): ₹30,000–₹80,000/month
- Unpaid internships: Legally valid in India but ethically questionable; interns are providing labor, and zero compensation is exploitative unless the learning value is exceptional and explicitly documented.

Red flag: Stipend below ₹10,000/month for any internship requiring full-time engagement in a metro city.
Red flag: Stipend paid inconsistently or subject to deductions not pre-agreed.

---

WHAT A GOOD INTERNSHIP AGREEMENT SHOULD CONTAIN

1. PARTIES AND DURATION:
   - Full name of intern and company.
   - Start and end date of the internship (specific dates, not "approximately 3 months").
   - Working hours per day and per week.

2. STIPEND:
   - Exact monthly stipend amount.
   - Payment date (typically end of month or beginning of next).
   - Any deductions (TDS if stipend > ₹50,000/month is not typical, but should be disclosed).
   - Expense reimbursement policy for work-related expenses.

3. ROLE AND SCOPE:
   - Title and department.
   - Nature of work / key responsibilities.
   - Supervisor / reporting manager name and title.

4. PRE-PLACEMENT OFFER (PPO) TERMS (if applicable):
   - Performance criteria for PPO offer.
   - When PPO will be communicated (ideally during internship, not after return to college).
   - PPO offer validity period.
   - If PPO is offered, whether it is binding on the company.

5. CONFIDENTIALITY:
   - What information is confidential.
   - Duration of confidentiality obligation (standard: 1–2 years for interns).
   - Carve-outs for publicly available information.

6. IP ASSIGNMENT:
   - Standard: Work produced during the internship for company projects belongs to the company.
   - Red flag: IP assignment clause covering personal projects or academic work done outside internship scope.

7. EARLY TERMINATION:
   - Notice period for intern to exit: Standard is 2 weeks for internships.
   - Company's right to terminate internship early: Should state grounds (misconduct, work not meeting standards) and notice.
   - Stipend for early termination: Pro-rated for work completed.

8. BENEFITS AND PROTECTIONS:
   - ESIC registration (required if intern earns ≤ ₹21,000/month and company is ESIC-covered).
   - Accident insurance (especially for interns traveling for work).
   - Workplace policies applicability: POSH (Sexual Harassment) policy should explicitly apply to interns.

---

CRITICAL: POSH ACT AND INTERNS

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 (POSH Act) explicitly includes interns in its definition of "aggrieved woman."

This means:
- Female interns are protected from sexual harassment in the workplace.
- Companies must include interns in their POSH training and awareness programs.
- The Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) must accept and process complaints from interns.
- Internship agreement should reference POSH compliance.

Red flag: Internship agreement that does not mention POSH applicability to interns.
Red flag: Company with no ICC — mandatory for all organizations with 10+ employees, including those with interns.

---

PPO (PRE-PLACEMENT OFFER) — RED FLAGS AND BEST PRACTICES

A PPO converts your internship into a full-time job offer.

Standard PPO process:
- Company evaluates performance during internship.
- PPO offer issued before or at the end of internship (not months after).
- PPO contains a written offer letter with CTC, role, joining date.
- Intern has a defined window (typically 1–3 weeks) to accept or decline.

Red flags in PPO terms:
- PPO "subject to business requirements" with no commitment — company can rescind without consequence.
- PPO joining date more than 6–9 months after graduation — creates uncertainty.
- PPO CTC significantly below market — take the time to benchmark before accepting.
- PPO validity period so short you cannot compare with campus placement offers properly.
- Company rescinds a confirmed PPO — this has happened in India (2022–2023 startup hiring freeze). You may have legal grounds for damages if you declined other offers based on a signed PPO.

---

WHAT INTERNS SHOULD NOT SIGN

Red flag: An internship agreement that contains:
- A multi-year non-compete post-internship.
- A bond requiring payment if you leave before the internship ends (bonds are for employees, not interns doing unpaid or low-paid work).
- IP assignment covering work done outside internship scope.
- A clause waiving POSH rights.
- Confidentiality so broad it covers everything you learn in your field of study.

---

RIGHTS YOU RETAIN REGARDLESS OF AGREEMENT

Even without a statute specifically for interns, Indian law provides:
1. Right not to be subjected to sexual harassment (POSH Act — applies to interns).
2. Right to payment of agreed stipend — breach of contract, recoverable in civil court.
3. Right to safe working conditions — general duty of care under law.
4. Right to ESIC coverage if earning ≤ ₹21,000/month in a covered establishment.
5. Right to receive any promised PPO in writing, if offered verbally.

---

KEY QUESTIONS FOR INTERNS TO ASK BEFORE SIGNING

1. What is the exact stipend amount? When is it paid?
2. What are the specific performance criteria for receiving a PPO?
3. If a PPO is offered, when will it be communicated, and what is the acceptance window?
4. Is the company ESIC-covered? Will I receive ESIC benefits?
5. What is the early termination notice period for both sides?
6. Does the confidentiality clause cover only work done for the company, or does it extend to my personal academic research?
7. Does the POSH policy apply to interns at this company? Is there an Internal Complaints Committee?
8. Is there any expense reimbursement for work-related travel, accommodation, or equipment?
