TOPIC: Notice Period Standards — Indian Employment Contracts
APPLICABLE TO: employment_contract, offer_letter

---

OVERVIEW

A notice period is the amount of time either party (employer or employee) must give before terminating employment. It protects both sides: the employer gets time to find a replacement, and the employee gets time to find a new job.

In India, notice periods are governed primarily by the terms of the employment contract. The Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946, and state Shops and Establishments Acts provide baseline protections for workers. For white-collar IT and service-sector employees, the contract terms typically dominate over statutory minimums.

As per the Shops and Establishments Act (most states): minimum 30 days' notice for employees with 1+ year of service. But contracts routinely override this upward.

---

STANDARD NOTICE PERIODS BY EXPERIENCE LEVEL

Fresher / Entry Level (0–2 years experience):
- Standard: 30 days (most product/startup companies)
- Common in IT services: 60 days
- Red flag: 90 days — disproportionately long for entry level with low institutional knowledge
- Hard red flag: More than 90 days — do not accept without strong negotiation or buyout clause

Junior to Mid Professional (2–5 years experience):
- Standard: 30–60 days
- Most common: 60 days across IT and product companies
- Acceptable in large enterprises: 90 days
- Red flag: More than 90 days

Mid-Level / Team Lead / Manager (5–10 years experience):
- Standard: 60–90 days
- Common in product companies (FAANG, Indian unicorns): 60 days
- Common in IT services (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL): 90 days
- Red flag: More than 90 days without corresponding garden leave or buy-out provision

Senior / Director / VP (10+ years):
- Standard: 90 days (3 months)
- Senior executives at MNCs: Up to 6 months, with garden leave
- Red flag: More than 6 months unless compensated heavily

C-Suite (CXO level):
- Standard: 3–6 months, often with full garden leave and ESOP/bonus protections

---

NOTICE PERIODS BY COMPANY TYPE (2024)

Startups (Pre-Series B):
- Typical: 30 days, sometimes 15 days
- Rationale: Startups need flexibility; they hire fast and expect to move fast

Startups (Series B and above):
- Typical: 30–60 days; some unicorns have moved to 60–90 days for senior roles

Indian IT Services — TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL, Tech Mahindra:
- Typical for freshers and juniors: 60 days (during probation), 90 days post-confirmation
- Industry pattern: These companies actively enforce full notice service; notice period buyout is allowed but at company discretion
- TCS specifically: Has pursued civil recovery suits in labor courts against employees who did not serve notice

Product Companies — Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta India:
- Typical: 30–60 days
- Often willing to negotiate to 30 days with buyout
- Amazon India: known for 2-month notice in most roles

Indian Product/Unicorns — Flipkart, Swiggy, Zomato, Razorpay, CRED:
- Typical: 30–60 days
- Most allow buyout

Consulting / Big 4 (Deloitte, EY, PwC, KPMG Tech):
- Typical: 60–90 days depending on level
- Senior managers and above: 90 days common

Banking and Financial Services (HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak, fintechs):
- Typical: 60–90 days
- Reason: Regulatory handover requirements (RBI, SEBI mandates) for certain roles require extended knowledge transfer
- Investment banking / trading: May require 90–180 days for proprietary book handover

Government and PSUs:
- Notice period: 3 months minimum; some roles require departmental approval for resignation

THE SYMMETRY PRINCIPLE

A fair notice period is symmetric: if the company can terminate you on 30 days' notice, your resignation notice should also be 30 days.

Common asymmetry red flag: Contract says company can terminate on 15 days' notice during probation, but employee must give 90 days' notice at any time.

This asymmetry is prevalent in Indian IT services contracts and gives the employer maximum flexibility while tying down the employee. Look for the termination notice clause separately from the resignation notice clause.

Always ask: What is the notice period on the company's side? Is it equal to mine?

---

NOTICE PERIOD BUYOUT

Notice period buyout = paying the company your remaining salary in lieu of serving the notice period.

Standard rate: (Monthly gross salary ÷ 30) × remaining notice days.
Example: ₹1,00,000/month CTC, 60-day notice, leaving after 30 days served → buyout = ₹50,000.

Standard practice: Buyout allowed in most modern contracts. Many companies allow both parties to initiate buyout.

Red flag: Buyout not allowed — you must physically serve the full notice period.
Red flag: Buyout allowed only at management's discretion — company can block your departure.
Red flag: Buyout rate is calculated on CTC (not gross), making it artificially expensive.

Pro tip: New employers often pay notice buyout as part of your joining offer. Negotiate this upfront. Put the amount in writing in the offer letter or appointment letter.

---

NOTICE PERIOD DURING PROBATION

Standard: 7–15 days of notice during probation (much shorter than post-confirmation).
Common: Immediate release or 7-day notice during probation.
Best practice: Probation notice should be clearly stated separately from the general notice clause.

Red flag: Full 90-day notice applies even during probation — unusually aggressive.
Red flag: No mention of probation notice — general notice clause applies from day one.

Note: If the probation notice is not separately defined in the contract, courts typically apply the general notice period. Always clarify this in writing before joining.

---

GARDEN LEAVE

Garden leave = you serve your notice period at home, without working, but continue to receive full salary.

Implications for employee:
- Positive: Paid salary while free to prepare for next role.
- Negative: Cannot legally join the new employer while on garden leave (risk of breach of contract).

Standard: Increasingly common for senior, technical, or client-facing roles with access to sensitive information.

If the company invokes garden leave, they must pay you for the full duration — including all salary, benefits, and vesting that would have occurred.

Red flag: Garden leave invoked but salary suspended — illegal. Garden leave without pay is not valid garden leave.

---

NOTICE PERIOD WAIVER (EARLY RELEASE)

A company can waive the notice period entirely, releasing you immediately with full notice pay.

Entitlement: Not automatic — this is the employer's discretion unless explicitly stated in the contract.

Practical reality: IT companies rarely waive notice for employees in critical projects. Startups and product companies frequently allow early release.

Managed exits (PIPs): If you are exited via a Performance Improvement Plan, companies often offer immediate release. This is negotiated, not contractual.

---

NOTICE PAY IN LIEU

Some contracts allow the company to pay you notice period salary instead of having you work it.

Standard: Allowed in most modern Indian employment contracts.
Used when: Company doesn't want you in the office during transition (e.g., after a resignation to a direct competitor).
Note: If the company pays you notice pay in lieu, your last working day is your resignation acceptance date, not 60/90 days later. This counts for PF, gratuity, and tax purposes.

---

KEY QUESTIONS TO ASK BEFORE SIGNING

1. What is the notice period from MY side vs the company's side? Are they symmetric?
2. Is buyout of notice period allowed? Who can initiate it — me, the company, or both?
3. What is the notice period during probation? Is it separately defined?
4. Can the company place me on garden leave? Do I receive full salary during garden leave?
5. Is there a specific clause about early release or notice period waiver?
6. How is the buyout amount calculated — on gross salary or CTC?
