TOPIC: Probation Periods and Termination Clauses — Indian Employment

---

OVERVIEW

Probation is a trial period at the start of employment during which both the employer and employee assess fit. During probation, the terms of termination and notice are usually different — and typically more favorable to the employer.

Understanding the probation clause is critical because it defines your legal protections and the risk of early exit.

---

PROBATION PERIOD STANDARDS

Standard probation duration in India:
- Entry level (freshers): 3–6 months
- Lateral hire (experienced): 3–6 months (commonly 3 months for senior roles)
- Most common across IT, BFSI, and consulting: 6 months
- Red flag: More than 12 months of probation — disproportionately long, signals a risk-averse employer or attempts to maintain lower protections longer.
- Red flag: Probation duration undefined — open-ended probation.

Probation confirmation:
- Standard: Automatic confirmation after probation period ends, unless employer issues a written non-confirmation notice.
- Red flag: Probation requires a separate written confirmation letter — if the company "forgets" or delays, you remain unconfirmed indefinitely.
- Best practice: Contract should state "confirmation is automatic unless a written extension or non-confirmation notice is issued before [date]."

Probation extension:
- Standard: Employer can extend probation once, for up to 3 months.
- Red flag: Unlimited probation extension at employer's discretion with no maximum.
- Red flag: No notice required before probation extension.

---

TERMINATION DURING PROBATION

Standard:
- Employer can terminate with 7–15 days' notice during probation.
- Employee must give the same (7–15 days) or sometimes less.

Red flag: Employee must give 90 days' notice even during probation (asymmetric).
Red flag: Immediate termination without notice or pay in lieu — permissible only for gross misconduct (defined clearly in the contract or standing orders).

Probation termination and PF: If you are terminated during probation, you are entitled to all PF contributions (both employee's and employer's share) credited to your EPF account. The employer cannot withhold PF.

Probation termination and gratuity: No gratuity payable — gratuity requires 5 years of continuous service.

Probation termination and notice pay: If terminated without notice, you are entitled to salary in lieu of notice (notice pay) unless the termination is for misconduct proven through a proper domestic inquiry.

---

POST-PROBATION TERMINATION (CONFIRMED EMPLOYEES)

For confirmed (post-probation) employees, termination protections are stronger:

Standard notice period from company: 30–90 days depending on role and contract.
Standard: Company must give written notice of termination AND reasons (for cause termination).
Retrenchment compensation: Under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, employees who have worked for 1+ year are entitled to retrenchment compensation of 15 days' average wages for each completed year of service, if terminated due to business reasons (redundancy, downsizing).

Note: The Industrial Disputes Act (IDA) primarily protects "workmen" — employees in roles not involving supervisory or managerial functions, typically earning ≤ ₹24,000/month basic. Many IT professionals may not qualify as "workmen" under IDA. However, the Shops and Establishments Act in most states offers additional protections to all employees.

---

PERFORMANCE IMPROVEMENT PLAN (PIP)

A PIP (Performance Improvement Plan) is a formal process used by companies to document poor performance before termination.

Standard PIP period: 30–90 days.
Standard practice: PIP is a precursor to termination. Very few employees are "successfully" exited out of a PIP with retention. Most PIPs are managed exits.

What your contract should say:
- Clear definition of performance standards that trigger a PIP.
- A minimum PIP duration before termination can follow.
- Right to respond in writing to PIP notice.
- No reduction in salary or benefits during PIP period.

PIP and full and final settlement (F&F): If you resign during a PIP, standard notice period applies unless waived. If terminated via PIP, notice pay must be given.

Red flag: Contract allows termination on "unsatisfactory performance" without a defined PIP process — you can be terminated without any structured warning.
Red flag: Contract says "performance will be evaluated at management's discretion" — subjective and employee-hostile.
Red flag: PIP can be initiated within first 30 days of joining — before any realistic performance assessment is possible.

---

TERMINATION FOR CAUSE

"For cause" termination = termination due to gross misconduct. Standard grounds include:
- Theft, fraud, or dishonesty.
- Serious breach of confidentiality or data security.
- Harassment, discrimination, or violence.
- Willful insubordination.
- Falsification of credentials, experience, or documents.

Standard due process:
1. Domestic inquiry / show cause notice to employee.
2. Employee's written response within defined period (typically 7–15 days).
3. Management decision based on inquiry findings.
4. Termination letter with documented reasons.

Red flag: Contract allows immediate termination for "misconduct" with no definition of what constitutes misconduct.
Red flag: Termination for cause with no domestic inquiry process — removes your right to defend yourself.
Red flag: Loss of all unpaid salary, PF, or pending benefits upon for-cause termination — illegal under Indian law. PF cannot be withheld regardless of reason for termination.

LAYOFFS AND REDUNDANCY IN INDIA

Mass layoffs in Indian IT have been significant since 2022–2023, with companies like Byju's, Unacademy, ShareChat, Ola, and even large companies like Infosys BPM and Wipro undertaking restructuring.

Legal framework for layoffs:
- Establishments with 100+ workmen: Must obtain government permission before retrenchment (IDA Section 25O). This applies to factories and manufacturing; IT companies typically argue they are "shops" under the Shops and Establishments Act and avoid this requirement.
- IT companies typically offer: 1–3 months' severance + notice pay. This is voluntary — no statutory obligation for most IT professionals.

Market practice for severance (2024, Indian IT/tech):
- Layoff with < 1 year service: 1 month notice pay
- 1–3 years service: 1–3 months pay
- 3–5 years service: 2–4 months pay + possible equity acceleration
- 5+ years service: 3–6 months pay + retrenchment compensation under IDA if applicable

Red flag: No severance clause in contract — company is only obligated to provide statutory minimum (which for many IT professionals is just notice pay).
Red flag: Severance conditioned on signing a broad release of all legal claims — read this carefully before signing.

---

FULL AND FINAL SETTLEMENT (F&F)

F&F = the final payment settlement after you leave.

Standard components of F&F:
1. Salary for days worked in the final month.
2. Leave encashment (earned/privileged leaves not taken).
3. Notice period salary (if worked) OR notice pay in lieu.
4. Variable/incentive pay due as per performance (often pro-rated).
5. Expense reimbursements pending.
6. PF contributions (employer share released to EPF account — not paid in cash).
7. Gratuity (only if 5+ years of continuous service).

Standard F&F timeline: 30–60 days after last working day.
Red flag: F&F delayed beyond 60 days without clear explanation.
Red flag: F&F conditional on completion of "knowledge transfer" with no defined deadline — company can delay indefinitely.
Red flag: Leave encashment not paid during F&F — check your state's Shops and Establishments Act; most mandate this.

KEY QUESTIONS TO ASK BEFORE SIGNING

1. What is the probation period? Is confirmation automatic or does it require a separate letter?
2. Can the probation period be extended? How many times, and by how much?
3. What is the notice period during probation vs after confirmation?
4. What are the grounds for termination for cause? Is a domestic inquiry mandated?
5. Does the contract describe the PIP process, or can I be terminated without any warning?
6. What does the F&F settlement include, and within what timeline is it paid?
7. Does the company have a severance policy for layoffs? What is the calculation?
