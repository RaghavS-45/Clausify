OVERVIEW

Leave and benefits are a critical but often overlooked part of your total compensation. In India, several types of leave and benefits are mandated by law; others are discretionary. This KB covers both statutory requirements and market benchmarks so you know what is standard versus what is exceptional.

---

STATUTORY LEAVE ENTITLEMENTS (MANDATORY UNDER INDIAN LAW)

1. EARNED LEAVE / PRIVILEGED LEAVE (EL/PL)
   Statutory minimum: Under the Factories Act, 1948 — 1 day for every 20 days worked (for factory workers).
   For IT/service sector employees (Shops and Establishments Act):
   - Most states: 12–18 days earned leave per year.
   - Karnataka: 12 days EL per year (Karnataka Shops and Establishments Act)
   - Maharashtra: 21 days EL per year (Maharashtra Shops and Establishments Act)
   - Delhi: 15 days EL per year
   - Tamil Nadu: 12 days per year for employees with 240+ days of service
   Industry benchmark (IT/product companies): 18–24 days earned leave per year.
   Red flag: Less than 12 days EL per year — below statutory minimum in most states.

2. CASUAL LEAVE (CL)
   Not a statutory requirement in all states, but universally practiced.
   Standard: 7–12 days casual leave per year.
   Note: CL is typically non-encashable and non-carry-forward.

3. SICK LEAVE / MEDICAL LEAVE (SL)
   Statutory minimum varies by state and establishment type.
   Typical statutory minimum: 7–12 days per year (state-specific).
   Standard in Indian IT/product companies: 7–12 days.
   Note: Sick leave is typically non-encashable. Some companies convert unused sick leave to earned leave.

4. MATERNITY LEAVE
   Legal basis: Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act, 2017.
   Entitlement: 26 weeks paid maternity leave for the first two children.
   For third child onwards: 12 weeks.
   Adoption leave: 12 weeks for mothers adopting a child below 3 months old.
   Work from home (creche provisions): Companies with 50+ employees must provide a crèche facility or equivalent.
   Red flag: Contract states "maternity leave as per company policy" without referencing the Maternity Benefit Act — verify compliance.
   Red flag: Any attempt to reduce maternity leave below 26 weeks for first/second child is illegal.

5. PATERNITY LEAVE
   No central statute mandates paternity leave in the private sector.
   PSUs / Central Government: 15 days paternity leave.
   Industry benchmark (IT/product companies 2024): 2–4 weeks.
   Red flag: No mention of paternity leave at all in a modern tech company.
   Progressive standard: 4–8 weeks paternity leave (Zomato, Swiggy, Meesho have offered 8+ weeks).

6. BEREAVEMENT LEAVE
   Not mandated by any statute — entirely discretionary.
   Standard in IT/product companies: 3–5 days for immediate family (spouse, children, parents, siblings).
   Red flag: No mention of bereavement leave in the contract — must use earned leave even in a family emergency.

7. PUBLIC HOLIDAYS / GAZETTED HOLIDAYS
   Legal basis: Negotiable Instruments Act defines days of closure; each state has its own list of public holidays.
   Standard: 10–15 public holidays per year (combination of National and state holidays).
   Typically includes: Republic Day, Holi, Eid, Independence Day, Gandhi Jayanti, Diwali, Christmas, and state-specific festivals.
   Note: Many IT companies allow "restricted holidays" — employees choose additional holidays from a list of 5–6 optional festivals.

---

LEAVE CARRYOVER AND ENCASHMENT

Carryover standard: Most companies allow carry-forward of unused EL up to a maximum (typically 30–45 days).
Encashment on exit: Under most state Shops and Establishments Acts, earned leave can be encashed at the time of leaving. This is usually paid as part of Full and Final (F&F) settlement.
Encashment during employment: Some companies allow leave encashment once a year for leaves beyond the carry-forward limit. Taxable as salary income.

Red flag: "Leave lapsed if not used by year-end" — no carryover and no encashment. You lose the value of your earned leave entirely.
Red flag: Leave encashment on exit not mentioned — may lead to disputes during F&F.

ADDITIONAL STANDARD BENEFITS IN INDIAN IT/TECH (2024)

HEALTH INSURANCE (MEDICLAIM):
Standard: Group Mediclaim policy ≥ ₹3 lakh per employee. Top companies offer ₹5–10 lakh family floater.
Extended family coverage: Spouse, up to 2 children standard. Parents at additional premium or employer-covered — ask specifically.
Dental and OPD: Separate dental or OPD (Outpatient Department) cover is offered by progressive companies. Not universal.
Red flag: Sum insured < ₹3 lakh — inadequate for hospitalizations in tier-1 cities.
Red flag: Insurance conditional on completing 3 months — you are uninsured in a critical period.

TERM LIFE INSURANCE / GROUP LIFE COVER:
Standard: 3–5× annual CTC coverage.
Good practice: ₹50 lakh–₹1 crore flat life cover.
Often bundled: Many group health policies include group personal accident cover.

GRATUITY:
Statutory: Payable after 5 years of continuous service under Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972.
Formula: 15 days × last drawn basic salary × years of service / 26.
Funded vs unfunded: Large companies fund gratuity through LIC or private insurance. Small companies may pay from cash flow — risk of non-payment if company closes.

PROVIDENT FUND (EPF):
Employee: 12% of Basic deducted from salary.
Employer: 12% of Basic — of which 8.33% goes to EPS (Employee Pension Scheme), capped at ₹1,250/month; 3.67% to EPF.
Total EPF (employee + employer): 24% of Basic annually.
Interest rate: EPF interest for FY 2023-24: 8.25% per annum. Decided by EPFO each year.
Withdrawal: Full PF withdrawal allowed after 2 months of unemployment or at retirement.
Red flag: Employer not depositing PF contributions on time — you can check your EPF balance via EPFO portal (epfindia.gov.in). Delayed deposits mean lost interest.

FOOD AND MEAL BENEFITS:
Standard in IT: Subsidized cafeteria meals or food wallet (e.g., Sodexo, National MEAL Pass, Pluxee).
Annual food allowance (tax-exempt): ₹26,400/year (₹50 per meal, 2 meals per day, 22 working days per month).
This allowance is exempt from income tax under the old tax regime.

TRANSPORT / COMMUTE:
Standard: Company cab/shuttle for employees working late or in shifts.
WFH internet allowance: ₹1,000–₹2,000/month — increasingly common post-COVID. Partially tax-exempt.

PHONE AND EQUIPMENT ALLOWANCE:
Standard in tech roles: Company-provided laptop. Mobile phone allowance (₹500–₹2,000/month) for senior roles or those on client-facing duties.

EDUCATION AND LEARNING:
Standard at product/startup companies: ₹30,000–₹1,00,000/year learning budget for courses, conferences, books.
Reimbursed on proof of enrollment or completion.
Red flag: No learning budget mentioned in offer — especially relevant for rapidly evolving tech roles.

WORK FROM HOME / REMOTE WORK:
Post-pandemic market standard: Hybrid work (2–3 days in office) has become the norm at most large Indian IT and product companies (2024).
Full WFH: Some companies (especially distributed tech startups, international product companies) offer full remote work.
Red flag: Contract prohibits WFH entirely with no discretion — rigid in a hybrid work era.
Red flag: WFH policy "subject to change at company's discretion" — can be revoked without notice.

INTERNSHIP-SPECIFIC BENEFITS

For internship agreements:
Standard stipend (FY 2024, India):
- Engineering internships at top product companies: ₹40,000–₹1,20,000/month
- Startup internships: ₹10,000–₹40,000/month
- IT services internships (TCS, Infosys): ₹10,000–₹15,000/month
- MBA/management internships: ₹30,000–₹80,000/month
Pre-placement offer (PPO): Defined criteria for PPO conversion should be in writing.
Benefits during internship: ESIC coverage required for interns earning < ₹21,000/month (as per ESI Act amendment 2023).

---

KEY QUESTIONS TO ASK ABOUT BENEFITS

1. What is the earned leave / privileged leave entitlement per year?
2. What is the health insurance coverage — sum insured, does it cover family, are parents included?
3. Is there a learning / training allowance? What is the amount and approval process?
4. What is the WFH / remote work policy? Is it contractual or discretionary?
5. Is there a group term life insurance? What is the cover amount?
6. How is unused earned leave handled at year-end — carryover or lapse?
7. Is leave encashment provided on resignation or only on retirement?
8. What is the company's paternity/secondary caregiver leave policy?
