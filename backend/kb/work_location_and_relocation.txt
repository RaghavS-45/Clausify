OVERVIEW

Work location clauses define where you are required to physically work. In the post-COVID era, these clauses have become more complex and more important. Companies now often include hybrid work expectations, relocation rights, and transfer clauses that can significantly affect your career and personal life.

This KB covers the legal framework, market standards for 2024, and red flags to watch for.

---

STANDARD WORK LOCATION CLAUSE

A standard clause specifies:
1. Your primary work location (city/office/work-from-home).
2. Whether you can be relocated to another city or country.
3. How much notice must be given before relocation.
4. Whether relocation is voluntary or mandatory.

Acceptable standard:
"The primary place of work is [City]. The company reserves the right to transfer the employee to any of its offices in India, with reasonable notice of [60–90 days], and relocation assistance."

Red flag: "Employee agrees to work at such locations as the company may direct, within India or abroad, without any additional notice or compensation."
→ No notice period, no assistance, international deployment at will — extremely aggressive.

---

WORK-FROM-HOME / HYBRID WORK (2024 MARKET STANDARD)

Post-pandemic WFH landscape in India (2024):
Full in-office (5 days/week): TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL — have required full office return since 2023–2024.
Hybrid 3 days office: Amazon India, Google India, Microsoft India — standard for most roles.
Hybrid 2 days office: Meta India, many product companies and startups.
Full remote: Rare in large companies; more common in global-first startups with distributed teams.

SEBI regulation impact: SEBI issued guidelines in 2023 requiring broking firms and financial intermediaries to return to office. BFSI sector largely back to full office.

Key principle: Work-from-home arrangements are NOT automatically protected by law in India. They are contractual. If the contract says your work location is "Bangalore office," the company can require you to come in every day — even if you had an informal WFH arrangement for 2 years.

Get WFH/hybrid terms in writing in the appointment letter, not just verbally from your manager.

TRANSFER AND RELOCATION CLAUSES

A transfer clause allows the company to move you to a different city or office.

Standard transfer clause:
"The company may, with a notice period of 60–90 days, require the employee to transfer to another location within India. The company will provide reasonable relocation assistance."

Relocation assistance (2024 market benchmarks):
- Entry level (0–3 years): ₹25,000–₹75,000 one-time relocation allowance
- Mid level (3–8 years): ₹50,000–₹1,50,000 + temporary accommodation (2–4 weeks)
- Senior level (8+ years): ₹1,00,000–₹3,00,000 + temporary accommodation + house hunting trips
- International relocation: Visa support, family travel, temporary housing, settling-in allowance (typically 1–3 months salary)

Red flag: No relocation assistance mentioned — company can transfer you but won't pay for the move.
Red flag: Transfer notice is less than 30 days — insufficient to plan a life relocation.
Red flag: Transfer required even if spouse has a local job and children are in school — no personal circumstances exception.
Red flag: International transfer without visa support or cost of living adjustment.

---

INTERNATIONAL DEPLOYMENT / DEPUTATION

Many IT services companies and MNC subsidiaries deploy employees internationally.

Standard for international deployment:
- Advance notice: 30–90 days
- Per diem / international allowance: Standard for onsite (US: USD 60–90/day for accommodation; UK: GBP 50–70/day)
- Tax equalization: Company bears any tax arising from international deployment (standard at large MNCs and Big 4)
- Housing: Company-provided or subsidized housing during international assignment
- Return ticket: Company-paid travel both ways
- Family deputation: Typically allowed for assignments > 6 months (at employee's or employer's cost — varies)

Red flag: International deployment clause with no mention of per diem, housing, or travel support — implies "at your own expense."
Red flag: Contract requires unlimited international travel at zero notice.
Red flag: Tax equalization not mentioned — you could face double taxation (India + host country) without company support.

RETURN-TO-OFFICE MANDATES AND LEGAL STANDING

Can a company legally require you to return to the office after you have been working from home?

Short answer: YES, if your contract specifies an office location. Informal WFH arrangements (even if practiced for years) do not create a legal right to continue WFH.

However: If the company unilaterally changed your work location to WFH in writing (e.g., a COVID-era WFH policy in the appointment letter), any mandatory return may require amendment to the employment contract — which requires your consent.

Practical reality: Most RTO mandates in India are enforced through attendance tracking and performance pressure, not through disciplinary proceedings. Resistance is usually negotiated, not litigated.

NIGHT SHIFT, SHIFT ROTATION, AND UNSOCIABLE HOURS

Relevant for BPO, banking operations, healthcare tech, and logistics companies.

Statutory protections for women in night shifts:
- Factories Act, 1948: Section 66 restricts women from working between 7 PM and 6 AM in factories (state-specific amendments exist).
- Several states (Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu) allow women in night shifts at IT/IT-enabled services establishments with specific safety conditions (transport, security).
- Safety requirements for night shifts: Company transport or reimbursement, security escort, safe premises.

Shift allowance:
- Standard for night shift (9 PM–6 AM): ₹1,000–₹3,000/month additional.
- Red flag: Night shift work required with no shift allowance and no transport support.

Shift rotation:
- Standard notice before shift change: 1–4 weeks.
- Red flag: No notice required before changing employee's shift — disruptive to personal planning.

---

WORK FROM HOME ALLOWANCES AND TAX

Broadband / internet allowance: Partially tax-exempt — company reimbursement of documented internet bills is typically non-taxable as perquisite.

Electricity and utility allowances: Not specifically exempt; treated as taxable perquisite unless covered under a structured flexible benefit plan.

Home office equipment: Company-provided laptop/equipment used from home — not taxable if ownership remains with company.

Mobile allowance: Standard for senior/client-facing roles — ₹500–₹2,000/month. Partially tax-exempt if tied to business use.

SPECIFIC CLAUSES TO WATCH FOR

1. "Employee may be required to work from client premises."
   → Common in consulting and IT services. Acceptable, but check:
   - Is the client location far from your stated work location?
   - Who pays for commuting to client sites?
   - Does the client assignment come with an allowance?

2. "Employee agrees that their work location may be changed without notice."
   → Hard red flag. Any location change that requires you to move should come with reasonable notice.

3. "WFH is a privilege, not a right, and may be revoked at the company's discretion."
   → This is legally accurate in most Indian contracts. Acceptable if WFH is genuinely informal. Not acceptable if you specifically negotiated WFH as part of your offer.

4. "Employee will not be entitled to any additional compensation for remote work."
   → Standard. But cross-check whether broadband and phone reimbursements are available.

---

KEY QUESTIONS TO ASK BEFORE SIGNING

1. What is my primary work location as stated in the contract?
2. What is the company's WFH or hybrid policy? Is it contractual or discretionary?
3. Can the company transfer me to another city? What is the notice period and relocation assistance?
4. If I am deployed to a client site, is there any additional allowance or commuting support?
5. Is international deployment a possibility in this role? What support is provided (per diem, housing, tax equalization, family travel)?
6. If my work location is "home," what does the company provide — laptop, internet allowance, chair/desk budget?
7. For shift roles: What is the shift schedule, shift allowance, and transport support for late/night shifts?
