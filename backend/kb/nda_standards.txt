DOCUMENT TYPE: Knowledge Base Reference
TOPIC: NDA Standards and Non-Disclosure Agreement Clauses — India
APPLICABLE TO: nda

---

OVERVIEW

A Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) is a legal contract that restricts one or both parties from disclosing confidential information to third parties. In the employment context, NDAs appear as:
(a) Standalone pre-employment NDAs signed before interviews or before sharing proprietary information.
(b) Embedded confidentiality clauses within employment agreements.
(c) Post-employment NDAs signed at exit alongside full and final settlement.

Indian NDAs are governed by the Indian Contract Act, 1872. Confidentiality obligations are generally enforceable in India, unlike non-compete clauses (which are largely void post-employment).

---

TYPES OF NDAs

1. UNILATERAL NDA: Only one party (usually the employee) is restricted from disclosing. Most common in employment.
2. BILATERAL/MUTUAL NDA: Both parties agree not to disclose each other's confidential information. Common in partnerships, vendor agreements.
3. MULTILATERAL NDA: Three or more parties. Used in consortium or joint venture contexts.

Standard in employment: Unilateral NDA — the employee is bound; the company is not.
Red flag: A mutual NDA in an employment context that restricts employees from disclosing company wrongdoing.

---

WHAT IS STANDARD IN AN EMPLOYMENT NDA

Confidential information definition (standard):
"All non-public information relating to the company's technology, products, business plans, client relationships, financial data, pricing, and trade secrets that is disclosed by the company to the employee in the course of employment."

Appropriate inclusions: source code, unreleased product specs, client lists, sales pipeline data, internal pricing, strategic plans, proprietary research.

Appropriate exclusions (standard carve-outs — always look for these):
(a) Information that is or becomes publicly available through no fault of the employee.
(b) Information the employee knew prior to employment (with proof).
(c) Information independently developed by the employee without use of company's confidential information.
(d) Information received from a third party not under any confidentiality obligation.
(e) Information required to be disclosed by law, court order, or regulatory authority.

Red flag: No carve-outs — everything is defined as confidential indefinitely.

---

DURATION OF CONFIDENTIALITY OBLIGATIONS

Standard for trade secrets: Indefinite — trade secrets can be protected permanently as long as they remain secret.
Standard for other business information: 2–5 years post-employment.
Standard for general employment information: 1–2 years post-employment.

Red flag: Lifetime confidentiality obligation for non-trade-secret information (e.g., "Employee shall never discuss the company's client relationships" — this restricts career networking indefinitely).

Red flag: Shorter duration with a vague definition of "trade secret" — company may argue everything is a trade secret and enforce indefinitely.

---

SCOPE OF NDA — RED FLAGS

Red flag 1 — Overly broad "confidential information" definition:
"All information related to the company's business, strategy, operations, or any information the company designates as confidential."
→ Designation-based definitions mean the company can retroactively label anything as confidential.

Red flag 2 — Restrictions on future employment:
"Employee shall not use any knowledge or skills gained during employment in any future role."
→ This is effectively an unenforceable non-compete dressed as a confidentiality clause. Skills acquired through employment belong to the employee.

Red flag 3 — Whistleblower gag clause:
"Employee shall not disclose any information about the company's business practices to any government authority, regulatory body, or media."
→ This clause is likely unenforceable and potentially illegal. The right to report legal violations to regulatory authorities (SEBI, RBI, MCA, labor commissioners) cannot be contractually waived.

Red flag 4 — Salary and compensation confidentiality:
"Employee shall not disclose the terms of their compensation to any third party."
→ Salary transparency is increasingly recognized as important for pay equity. While technically permissible under Indian contract law, courts may not enforce penalties for salary disclosure among employees.

Red flag 5 — Blanket IP assignment within the NDA:
"Any inventions conceived by the employee during the NDA period are the property of the company."
→ Mixing IP assignment into the NDA unnecessarily expands the scope beyond confidentiality.

---

REMEDIES FOR BREACH OF NDA

In India, a party that breaches an NDA can face:
1. Civil suit for breach of contract — damages under Section 73 of the Indian Contract Act.
2. Injunction — court order preventing further disclosure or use of confidential information. Courts can grant interim injunctions quickly.
3. Specific performance — enforcement of the NDA obligation.
4. Criminal liability — if the breach constitutes theft of trade secrets under the Information Technology Act, 2000 (Section 43) or Indian Penal Code (Section 406 — criminal breach of trust).

Penalty clauses in NDAs:
- Standard: Specific monetary penalties (₹5–50 lakh range) for documented breach.
- Red flag: Penalties without a requirement to prove actual damage — under Indian law (Section 74), courts can reduce unconscionable penalties.
- Red flag: Criminal complaint threatened for any breach — most NDA breaches are civil in nature; criminal threats are coercive tactics.

---

STANDALONE PRE-EMPLOYMENT NDA (BEFORE JOINING)

These are signed before interviews or during due diligence.

Standard:
- Covers only specific information shared during the interview/evaluation process.
- Duration: 12–24 months from signing.
- Covers only information received in written form or marked "confidential."

Red flag: Pre-employment NDA that attempts to assign IP rights for anything you create during the evaluation period — even as a response to a technical assessment.

Red flag: Pre-employment NDA signed as a condition of interview that has broader scope than what is needed for the interview process.

---

EXIT NDA / SEPARATION AGREEMENT

Many companies ask departing employees to sign an NDA or confidentiality reaffirmation as part of the full and final settlement process.

Standard components:
- Reaffirmation of existing confidentiality obligations.
- Confirmation that all company materials (devices, documents, code) have been returned.
- Acknowledgement that post-employment restrictions apply.
- Non-disparagement clause (standard: both parties agree not to make damaging public statements about each other).

Red flag: Separation NDA includes new, broader restrictions not present in the original employment agreement.
Red flag: Separation NDA requires waiving all legal claims — particularly claims for unpaid wages, harassment, or discrimination — as a condition for receiving full and final settlement. This is coercive. In India, statutory payments (PF, gratuity) cannot be withheld as leverage for signing a release.

---

NON-DISPARAGEMENT CLAUSES

Non-disparagement = agreement not to make negative public statements about the other party.

Standard: Mutual — both employee and company agree not to disparage each other.
Acceptable: Prevents employee from making false statements about the company.
Red flag: One-sided — only employee is restricted; company can say whatever it wants.
Red flag: Defined so broadly it prevents honest online reviews (Glassdoor, LinkedIn) — unlikely to be fully enforceable.
Red flag: Perpetual non-disparagement with criminal penalties for breach — disproportionate.

---

KEY QUESTIONS TO ASK BEFORE SIGNING AN NDA

1. What specific categories of information are defined as confidential? Is there a carve-out for publicly available information?
2. How long does the confidentiality obligation last after employment ends?
3. Does the NDA restrict me from reporting legal violations to regulatory authorities?
4. Does the confidentiality clause cover my salary — and if so, why?
5. Is the NDA mutual? Why is it unilateral if we are sharing information both ways?
6. Are there specific monetary penalties for breach? How are they calculated?
7. Does the NDA include any IP assignment provisions? Why?
