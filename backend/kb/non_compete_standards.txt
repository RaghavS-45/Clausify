---

OVERVIEW

Non-compete clauses restrict an employee from working for a competitor after leaving the company. Non-solicitation clauses restrict an employee from poaching clients or colleagues after leaving.

In India, post-employment non-compete clauses are largely unenforceable. Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, states that "every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade, or business of any kind is to that extent void."

However, ignoring these clauses entirely is risky. Companies use them to intimidate employees, file injunctions, and in connected industries, damage professional reputations.

---

LEGAL STATUS OF NON-COMPETE IN INDIA

POST-EMPLOYMENT NON-COMPETE:
Status: Generally void and unenforceable under Section 27, Indian Contract Act, 1872.

Key Supreme Court precedents:
- Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning & Manufacturing Co. (1967, SC): SC upheld non-compete clauses during employment duration only. Post-employment restriction was held void as a restraint of trade.
- Superintendence Company of India v. Krishan Murgai (1980, SC): Distinguished between restraints that operate during employment (valid) vs post-employment (void).

High Court precedents:
- Percept D'Mark (India) Pvt. Ltd. v. Zaheer Khan (2006, Bombay HC): Bombay HC declined to enforce post-employment non-compete against cricketer Zaheer Khan. Confirmed Section 27 void status.
- Desiccant Rotors International Pvt. Ltd. v. Bappaditya Sarkar (2009, Delhi HC): Delhi HC held that post-employment restraint on using "knowledge or experience" was void — you cannot prevent someone from using their professional skills.
- Wipro Ltd. v. Beckman Coulter International (2006, Delhi HC): While limited-time non-solicitation of specific clients was upheld, broad non-compete was not.

---

DURING-EMPLOYMENT NON-COMPETE:
Status: Fully enforceable.
Scope: You cannot work for a direct competitor, freelance for competing clients, or take equity in a competing company while employed.
Moonlighting: Working for a second employer (even in a different domain) without disclosure is typically a disciplinary violation.

---

NON-SOLICITATION (POST-EMPLOYMENT):
Status: More likely to be enforced than non-compete, particularly for specific client relationships.
Courts have upheld non-solicitation of named clients with whom the employee had direct relationships, for limited time periods.
Standard: 6–12 months, limited to clients you personally managed or served.
Red flag: "All present and prospective clients of the company" — prospective clients are unknown and unlimited; this is unenforceable.

---

NON-DISCLOSURE (PERMANENT, POST-EMPLOYMENT):
Status: Fully enforceable.
Covers: Trade secrets, customer lists, pricing data, proprietary algorithms, and unreleased product information — even post-employment, as long as the information remains confidential.
Cannot cover: General industry skills and knowledge acquired through experience, publicly available information.

---

STANDARD NON-COMPETE CLAUSE PARAMETERS

DURATION (post-employment, if included):
- Standard: 6 months — courts are more likely to read this as reasonable.
- Acceptable: Up to 12 months for senior roles (VP, CTO, key account manager) with genuine trade secret access.
- Red flag: More than 12 months — rarely upheld in India.
- Hard red flag: Indefinite or "as long as the information remains confidential" — void.

GEOGRAPHIC SCOPE:
- Standard: Specific named cities or the territory where the employee actually worked.
- Acceptable: All-India for companies with national client coverage.
- Red flag: Worldwide — unenforceable and excessive.
- Red flag: No geographic scope specified — implies unlimited.

INDUSTRY SCOPE:
- Standard: Restriction limited to direct product/service competitors in the same market segment.
- Acceptable: A named list of specific competitor companies.
- Red flag: "Any company in the technology/financial/healthcare sector" — far too broad.
- Red flag: "Any business whose products compete with any current or future product of the company" — future products are undefined; this is unlimited in scope.

COMPENSATION DURING NON-COMPETE:
- Best global practice: Company pays a "garden salary" during the non-compete period.
- Indian standard: No compensation paid during post-employment non-compete.
- Impact on enforceability: Courts view uncompensated post-employment restrictions even more skeptically.

---

NON-SOLICITATION CLAUSE PARAMETERS

CLIENT NON-SOLICITATION:
- Standard duration: 6–12 months post-employment.
- Standard scope: Clients you personally managed, not all company clients.
- Red flag: "All current or prospective clients" — impossibly broad.
- Red flag: Duration exceeding 24 months.

EMPLOYEE NON-SOLICITATION:
- Standard: 6–12 months post-employment.
- Standard scope: Employees you directly managed or worked closely with.
- Red flag: Blanket restriction on approaching any of the company's ~10,000 employees — over-broad.
- Red flag: Preventing casual professional contact (LinkedIn connection requests) — unenforceable micromanagement.

---

RED FLAG LANGUAGE TO WATCH FOR

"The employee shall not, directly or indirectly, engage in any business activity that competes with or is similar to the company's business for a period of [X years] anywhere in the world."
→ Void under Section 27. But signals an aggressive legal posture. Be cautious about the company's culture.

"'Competing business' means any entity that offers products or services that are the same as or similar to the company's."
→ "Similar to" is dangerously broad. Similar to Zomato? Any food delivery app. Similar to Zerodha? Any fintech.

"The employee shall not engage any current or prospective client of the company."
→ "Prospective" means anyone the company has ever pitched. Effectively bars you from the industry.

"This restriction applies even if the employee is terminated without cause."
→ You can be fired, receive no severance, and still be held to a non-compete. Extremely unfair. More enforceable than a voluntary-resignation-only clause, but courts disfavor this.

---

MOONLIGHTING CLAUSES IN INDIA

Moonlighting = working for a second employer simultaneously without disclosure.

Standard prohibition: Working for direct competitors during employment — fully enforceable.
Standard disclosure requirement: Informing employer of any outside paid engagement.

Industry positions (2024):
- Wipro: Terminated employees caught moonlighting (2022 incident — 300+ employees).
- Infosys: Issued advisory against moonlighting.
- Swiggy: Launched formal moonlighting policy allowing outside work with HR disclosure.
- Paytm: Allowed moonlighting with manager approval.
- Many startups and product companies: Allow with disclosure.

Red flag: Clause banning ALL outside work — even unrelated freelancing or teaching — without exception. Courts may not enforce a clause that prevents an employee from all economic activity outside working hours.

Best practice if you want to moonlight: Get written approval from your employer BEFORE starting. Keep it on personal time. Never use company resources. Avoid clients in overlapping domains.

---

WHAT TO DO IF YOUR CONTRACT HAS A BROAD NON-COMPETE

1. Ask for it to be removed — post-employment non-competes are void, and progressive employers (especially startups) will simply delete the clause on request.

2. Ask for it to be narrowed — specific named competitors, specific geography, shorter duration (6 months maximum).

3. Ask for compensation during the non-compete period — without pay, enforceability decreases further.

4. Document that you are not taking any confidential documents, code, databases, or client data when you leave. This removes the company's practical basis for enforcement.

5. Know that Indian courts rarely grant ex-parte injunctions against departing employees; getting a permanent injunction requires proving actual harm, not just contractual breach.

6. Consult a lawyer if you receive a legal notice — many legal threats from employers are tactical intimidation. An employment lawyer can assess the real risk in your specific situation.

---

KEY QUESTIONS TO ASK BEFORE SIGNING

1. Is there a non-compete clause? What is the duration and geographic scope?
2. Is "competing business" specifically defined? Can you narrow it to named companies?
3. Does the non-compete apply if the company terminates me without cause?
4. Is there compensation provided during the non-compete period?
5. Is the moonlighting restriction limited to working hours and competing businesses, or is it a blanket outside-work ban?
6. Is there a non-solicitation clause? What is the duration and who does it cover?
