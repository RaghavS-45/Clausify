OVERVIEW

An offer letter is the first binding (or semi-binding) document in your employment relationship. Most people sign it quickly without reading carefully. This document covers the most common and impactful red flags found in Indian offer letters — particularly in IT, consulting, fintech, and startup sectors.

This knowledge base entry covers red flags across compensation, clauses, conditions, and structure.

---

COMPENSATION RED FLAGS

Red flag 1 — CTC shown as a single number without a breakup:
"Your CTC is ₹10,00,000 per annum."
→ No breakup means you cannot evaluate the actual take-home. Request a full cost-to-company statement before signing. A well-structured offer always shows: Basic, HRA, Special Allowance, Variable Pay, PF, Gratuity, Insurance.

Red flag 2 — Large variable pay component at entry or junior level:
→ Variable pay above 20% at entry level means a significant portion of your CTC is at-risk and not guaranteed.
→ Ask: What are the performance targets? What percentage of employees typically receive 100% of variable pay? Has variable pay been paid in full in the last 3 years?

Red flag 3 — "Salary is inclusive of all statutory benefits":
→ This phrasing often means the employer is avoiding paying PF, gratuity, or health insurance on top of the stated salary. Under Indian law, PF contributions are mandatory (if salary > ₹15,000/month for shops and establishments). An employer claiming PF is "included in CTC" is not paying you more — they are simply accounting for their legal obligation within your stated CTC.

Red flag 4 — Joining bonus with aggressive clawback:
→ Clawback period exceeding 18 months is unusual.
→ Clawback at full amount even if partial period served — should be pro-rated.
→ Clawback triggered by any resignation, including for a legitimate reason like hostile work environment.

Red flag 5 — ESOP grant stated in offer letter without the full ESOP policy:
→ "You will receive 10,000 options as per the company's ESOP policy" — meaningless without knowing the vesting schedule, exercise price, exercise window, and total cap table.

Red flag 6 — "Salary revision will be based on performance and company policy" — no frequency commitment:
→ Standard: Annual appraisal with a documented process. No commitment to annual revision is a red flag.

---

CLAUSE RED FLAGS

Red flag 7 — 90-day notice period for entry-level or junior roles:
→ A 3-month notice period for a fresher or 0–3 year experience professional is abnormally long. Standard is 30–60 days.
→ This effectively locks you in for an extra 2 months beyond what the market norm requires.

Red flag 8 — Asymmetric notice period:
→ "Company can terminate with 15 days' notice; employee must give 90 days' notice."
→ This is unfair and exploitative. Negotiate for symmetry.

Red flag 9 — Employment bond without training cost disclosure:
→ "Employee shall pay ₹1,00,000 if they resign within 2 years" — without any explanation of what training this covers.
→ A bond without documented training investment is likely a punitive clause, not a genuine cost recovery.

Red flag 10 — IP clause covering personal projects and after-hours work:
→ "All inventions developed by the employee at any time during their employment belong to the company."
→ "At any time" means your personal projects, weekend side-projects, and unrelated creative work could be claimed.
→ Request a carve-out: "Inventions developed on personal time, using personal resources, and unrelated to the company's business are excluded."

Red flag 11 — Overly broad non-compete clause:
→ Post-employment non-competes are largely void under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act. However, an aggressive clause may be used to intimidate.
→ Red flag language: "Employee shall not work in the technology industry for 2 years after leaving."

Red flag 12 — Mandatory arbitration with unfavorable terms:
→ "All disputes shall be referred to arbitration in [city] with a sole arbitrator appointed by the company."
→ The company appointing the arbitrator creates a conflict of interest. Standard is a mutually agreed panel or arbitration under ICAI/DIAC rules.

---

CONDITION RED FLAGS

Red flag 13 — Conditional offer with vague conditions:
→ "This offer is subject to satisfactory background verification, document verification, and medical examination."
→ Undefined criteria — what does "satisfactory" mean? Can the company rescind the offer based on any background check finding?
→ Standard: Specific, defined conditions (e.g., "subject to clean criminal background check and verified educational credentials").

Red flag 14 — Offer contingent on providing competitor's salary slip:
→ Requiring you to produce a salary slip from your previous employer to "verify CTC" is an unfair negotiation tactic. In most cases, legally, you are not obligated to provide a previous employer's salary slip. Your offer should be based on the market rate for the role, not your previous salary.
→ This practice is increasingly challenged and has been banned in several US states. India has no specific ban, but you can decline to provide this.

Red flag 15 — Probation period that extends automatically:
→ "Probation will be for 6 months and may be extended at the company's discretion."
→ Without a cap on extensions, you could remain in probation with reduced protections indefinitely.
→ Standard: Probation extension should have a maximum (e.g., one extension of up to 3 months maximum).

Red flag 16 — Relocation clause without relocation assistance:
→ "Employee may be required to relocate to any company location in India or abroad."
→ No mention of relocation allowance, housing support, or transition time.
→ Standard: Relocation clause should include a reasonable notice period (60–90 days), relocation assistance (₹50,000–₹3 lakh range), and a right to refuse relocation for personal reasons.

STRUCTURAL RED FLAGS (FORMAT AND PROCESS)

Red flag 17 — Offer letter sent as image or non-editable PDF with 24-hour deadline:
→ Legitimate employers give you adequate time to review, consult a lawyer, and ask questions. A 24-hour deadline is coercive.
→ If an employer pressures you to sign immediately, that is itself a red flag about the work culture.

Red flag 18 — Critical terms communicated verbally but not in the offer letter:
→ "We'll give you a WFH option" or "Your target bonus is 100%" — but these are not written in the contract.
→ Verbal promises have no legal standing. Everything material must be in writing before you sign.

Red flag 19 — Role description is vague or absent:
→ "You will be assigned duties as determined by the management."
→ Without a defined role, scope, or reporting structure, your responsibilities can be changed arbitrarily.
→ Standard: Offer letter should state role title, department, reporting manager's title, and general scope of responsibilities.

Red flag 20 — Salary described as "₹X + performance incentive" without defining the incentive structure:
→ The incentive is part of your compensation — its conditions, targets, and measurement criteria must be documented.
→ "Performance incentive at management's discretion" effectively means it may never be paid.

Red flag 21 — No mention of health insurance:
→ Group health insurance is standard in virtually all formal employment in India. Its absence in an offer letter is a significant gap.
→ Ask: What is the sum insured? Does it cover family members? Who is the insurer?

Red flag 22 — Appointment letter to be issued "after joining" without specifying terms:
→ The offer letter is a summary; the appointment letter / employment agreement is the detailed contract.
→ You should see the appointment letter BEFORE joining, not after. Otherwise you are committed to the job without knowing the full terms.

---

WHAT GOOD OFFER LETTERS INCLUDE

A well-structured offer letter should contain:
✓ Full CTC breakup (Basic, HRA, Special Allowance, Variable, PF, Gratuity, Insurance)
✓ Clear definition of variable pay targets and payout conditions
✓ Joining date and location
✓ Probation period and confirmation process
✓ Notice period (both employer and employee, and whether buyout is allowed)
✓ Equity grant details (if applicable): number of options, exercise price, vesting schedule
✓ Any bond conditions with full disclosure of training costs
✓ Reference to the full appointment letter / employment contract to be provided before joining
✓ Benefits: Health insurance (sum insured, coverage), leaves entitlement, any other perks

---

QUESTIONS TO ASK BEFORE SIGNING AN OFFER LETTER

1. Can you share the full CTC breakup in a separate sheet?
2. What are the performance targets for the variable/bonus component?
3. What is the notice period on my side vs the company's side?
4. Is there an employment bond? What does the training cost cover?
5. Can I see the full appointment letter / employment agreement before signing the offer?
6. What are the conditions for background verification? What happens if a minor discrepancy is found?
7. Is the relocation clause optional or mandatory? What relocation support is provided?
8. What is the group health insurance coverage and who is included?
